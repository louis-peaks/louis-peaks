# Make Your Own Energy Gels

When you practise sport for longer periods, nutrition becomes an important part of training.

It is generally recommended to consume carbohydrates during activities lasting more than one hour. However, commercial energy gels can become expensive, especially if you train frequently or complete long sessions.

That is why I am sharing my homemade gel recipe, which can help you save up to 90 percent compared with commercial gels.

## Why carbohydrates matter

Your body needs energy, and carbohydrates are one of the fastest ways to provide it. One gram of carbohydrate provides approximately 4 calories.

During exercise, an amateur athlete can typically consume between 30 and 60 grams of carbohydrates per hour. Professional athletes may tolerate more than 100 grams per hour, but this requires specific training of the digestive system.

## The importance of glucose and fructose

Table sugar is made of glucose and fructose. Most athletes can absorb approximately 30 grams of fructose per hour before digestive problems may occur.

For this reason, endurance products usually combine different types of carbohydrates. Maltodextrin is made from glucose, while table sugar provides both glucose and fructose.

The ideal ratio is approximately 1 gram of fructose to 1.25 grams of glucose. This corresponds to a glucose to fructose ratio of about 1 to 0.8.

That is why I use table sugar together with maltodextrin. This provides more glucose than fructose and creates an inexpensive source of energy for long training sessions.
